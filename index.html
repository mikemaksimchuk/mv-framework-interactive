Before you assess

Four ideas, then you are ready

You do not need implementation science to use this. You do need these four things, because most wrong answers on a readiness tool come from missing one of them.

1 — Stages, not scores

You are not being graded

Districts move through stages over two to four years. A stage describes where the work is, not how good you are. Naming an early stage accurately is worth more than claiming a late one.

2 — Installation is real work

Deciding is not doing

Between "we decided to" and "students are using it" sits a whole stage: policy adopted, agreements signed, staff trained, procedures rehearsed. Skipping it is the most common way this fails. The 2023 framework did not have this column.

3 — Jagged is normal

Expect an uneven profile

You will be further along in some domains than others. Almost nobody sits in the same column across the board, and a flat profile usually means someone guessed. The uneven shape is the useful part.

4 — No best practice yet

The front end is design work

Implementation science assumes a proven practice to scale. With AI, nobody has one. So the early columns are deliberately messy: pilot, iterate, find out what works here. Discipline comes once something is worth locking in.
